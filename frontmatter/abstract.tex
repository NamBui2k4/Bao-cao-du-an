\chapter{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The selection of an appropriate software architecture plays a critical role in the development and 
evolution of \textbf{Learning Management Systems (LMS)} , directly affecting system scalability, maintainability, 
and adaptability to changing requirements. This project presents a comparative study of two widely 
adopted architectural approaches—\textbf{Monolithic},  and \textbf{Microservice}—within the context of 
an LMS application.

A core LMS system was designed with essential functionalities, including user management, course management,
enrollment, learning content delivery, and assessment handling. Based on the same functional requirements, 
the system was implemented separately using the two architectural styles to ensure a fair and consistent 
comparison. Several change and extension scenarios, such as modifying grading logic, adding learning 
progress statistics, and preparing for future learning analytics integration, were defined and applied
to each architecture.

The two architectures were evaluated across four quantitative dimensions: throughput and response time under 
variable load, CPU resource utilization efficiency (RUE), and development-phase availability derived from 
commit history analysis. The experimental results reveal a context-dependent performance hierarchy governed 
by a concurrency crossover threshold at approximately 1,000 Virtual Users. Below this threshold, 
the Monolithic architecture maintains measurable advantages due to the elimination of inter-service 
network hops, achieving a response time of 12.8 ms at single-user load compared to 34.1 ms for Microservices. 
Beyond the threshold, the performance hierarchy inverts: at 2,000 VUs under gradual ramp-up conditions, 
Microservices achieves 579.17 RPS versus the Monolith's 430.94 RPS — a 34.4\% superiority — and 
expands to a 63.2\% throughput advantage under simultaneous burst injection at 1,500 VUs. 
Resource efficiency analysis reveals that at normal operational load (200 VUs), the 
Microservices architecture delivers a CPU utilization efficiency (RUE\_CPU) of 1,148.09 
requests per CPU percentage point — approximately 2.0 times higher than the Monolith's 
573.22 — by processing 4.65 times more requests at comparable CPU expenditure. However, 
this efficiency advantage inverts under peak stress (1,000 VUs), where the Monolith's RUE\_CPU 
of 3,109.90 is 4.58 times higher, reflecting the exponential coordination overhead of eight 
simultaneously saturated containers. Fault tolerance analysis of 148 development-phase commit 
events demonstrates that the Microservices architecture achieved a system availability of 43.31\% 
compared to 35.14\% for the Monolith, attributable to its bounded blast-radius property that 
prevents localized service failures from propagating to unrelated functional domains. 
These findings provide a data-driven decision framework for selecting LMS architecture 
based on scale, operational load patterns, and organizational DevOps maturity.
